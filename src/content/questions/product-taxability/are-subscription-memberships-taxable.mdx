Membership taxability follows the substance of what's being provided. Access to services or community is generally not taxable; access to taxable goods or digital content may be.

## Key takeaways

- **Pure access memberships** (gym access, club membership, alumni association): generally not taxable, access rights and services aren't tangible personal property in most states
- **Digital content memberships** (streaming, ebook libraries, online courses): taxable in states that tax digital goods; not taxable in states that don't, same rules as standalone digital products
- **Software access memberships** (SaaS subscriptions, software seat licenses billed as memberships): treated as SaaS for taxability purposes, taxable in roughly half of US states
- **Physical goods memberships** (subscription boxes, curated product shipments): taxable: the physical goods delivered are taxable personal property, and the membership structure doesn't change that
- **Bundled memberships** with mixed benefits: most states apply a "true object" test (what is the primary purpose?) or require allocation between taxable and non-taxable components: this is one of the harder areas to handle correctly at scale
- **Washington state:** WA taxes digital automated services broadly and will capture most digital membership services
- **Annual vs. monthly billing:** the billing cadence doesn't change taxability, annual prepaid memberships and monthly memberships are treated the same way
- **Product tax codes:** assign PTCs based on what the membership delivers, not on the fact that it's a membership: the substance controls
