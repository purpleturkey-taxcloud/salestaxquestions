Multi-state exemption certificate management has two core challenges: getting the right form for each state, and maintaining records that hold up in audits across all of them. The SST uniform certificate solves the first problem for 24 states. The rest requires a systematic approach to form selection, collection, and retention.

## Step 1: Map your exempt buyers by state

For each buyer with exempt status, identify which states their purchases are sourced to. "Sourced to" means where the transaction is taxable, typically the ship-to address for shipped goods.

A buyer with locations in Ohio, Texas, and California who ships from you to all three needs certificates for all three states. The same buyer's Ohio purchases and Texas purchases are independent compliance events requiring independent certificates.

This mapping exercise is the foundation. Without knowing which state each transaction sources to, you can't know which certificate form you need.

## Step 2: Determine which certificate form each state accepts

**SST member states (24 states):** One form covers all of them. The [SST uniform exemption certificate](https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/for-businesses/documents/uniform-exemption-certificate) is accepted by all 24 SST member states. For buyers purchasing across multiple SST states, collect one SST certificate rather than 24 separate state forms. Valid for 5 years.

**Non-SST states that accept the MTC uniform certificate:** The Multistate Tax Commission's uniform sales and use tax exemption certificate is accepted by many non-SST states. Check the MTC's current acceptance list before relying on it for any specific state. States update their acceptance periodically.

**Non-SST states with their own required form:** The remaining non-SST states require their own state-specific form. Common examples:
- California: BOE-230 (resale) or CDTFA-230 series
- New York: Form ST-120 (resale)
- Florida: Form DR-13

Each state's Department of Revenue website provides the current version of its required form. Using an outdated form version may not satisfy the requirement.

## Step 3: Build a per-buyer, per-state certificate file

The audit-ready record structure:

| Customer | State | Form type | Issue date | Expiration date | Registration # verified | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acme Co. | PA (SST) | SST uniform | 2024-03-01 | 2029-03-01 | Yes | |
| Acme Co. | TX | TX-specific | 2024-03-01 | 2027-03-01 | Yes | |
| Acme Co. | CA | BOE-230 | 2024-03-01 | 2027-03-01 | Yes | |

Maintain this index alongside the actual certificate files. Digital storage is fine, scan paper certificates and store them associated with the customer account record.

## Step 4: Track expiration by state

Validity periods differ by state. SST states are 5 years. Some non-SST states are shorter — 1 to 3 years is common. Within a single customer relationship, you may have certificates expiring on different schedules in different states.

Set renewal reminders 60 to 90 days before each certificate's expiration. "One reminder per customer" doesn't work at multi-state scale: the reminder needs to be keyed to the state-specific expiration date.

## Step 5: Verify registration numbers at setup and periodically

When a buyer provides a certificate, verify their claimed registration number through the issuing state's DOR lookup tool before activating exempt treatment. Annual re-verification for high-volume accounts is a reasonable standard, registration permits can lapse without the buyer notifying you.

## Step 6: Maintain audit-ready storage

Auditors request certificates by transaction period and customer. The storage system needs to support:
- "Show me all exempt sales to Buyer X in 2023" → retrieve all certificates that were valid during 2023 for that buyer by state
- "Show me the certificate supporting this specific transaction" → trace from invoice to the certificate in effect on that date

Certificates covering past periods should be retained for 3 to 4 years after the last filing they supported, even after they've expired for future transactions.

## Software vs. manual

For sellers with a handful of B2B customers across a few states, a well-maintained spreadsheet index plus a shared drive of certificate PDFs is workable. For sellers with large B2B volumes or many states, certificate management software (standalone or integrated with your tax platform) automates the form routing, expiration tracking, and storage. The audit risk of manual tracking scales with complexity.
