Most sales tax platforms fall into one of two models: software you operate, or a service where a team does it for you. The right choice depends less on your business size and more on your ops capacity, risk tolerance, and how much of the compliance function you actually want to own.

## What each model actually means

**Sales tax software** (TaxCloud, TaxJar, Kintsugi, Avalara) handles the calculation, filing, and remittance automatically, but someone on your team needs to set it up, monitor it, and catch issues. Most platforms have AutoFile features that submit returns on a schedule without manual intervention. When something goes wrong (a missed state, a calculation error, a notice from a state) you're responsible for figuring it out, usually with a support ticket or a call.

**Managed compliance services** (Numeral, some offerings from accounting firms) put a team in charge of the entire function. Registration, filing, payment, state correspondence, it all goes through them. You get an account contact, a delivery guarantee, and in some cases a penalty protection clause. You're not monitoring anything; you're trusting the team.

## The cost trade-off

| | Software | Managed service |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical cost structure** | Monthly platform fee + per-filing fee or SST inclusion | Per-return fee ($75) + registration fee ($150/state) |
| **Cost at 5 states, monthly** | $200–500/month depending on platform and SST status | ~$375/month (Numeral: $75 × 5) |
| **Cost at 15 states, monthly** | Varies widely — SST enrollment changes the math significantly | ~$1,125/month ($13,500/year) |
| **SST program access** | Depends on platform, only CSP platforms offer this | Numeral is not a CSP; full fees regardless |
| **What goes up as you scale** | Per-return fees (unless SST-covered) | Returns × $75, every state, every period |

The cost gap widens at scale. Software platforms with CSP enrollment (like TaxCloud) cover SST-state filings through the program, so scaling into more SST states doesn't linearly increase cost. Managed services like Numeral charge $75/return regardless, so cost scales directly with filing volume.

## The ops capacity question

This is the real decision driver.

**Choose software if:**
- Someone on your team (a controller, bookkeeper, or ops person) can spend 1–2 hours per month on compliance oversight
- You want visibility into what's being filed and when
- You'd rather pay less and be involved than pay more and be fully hands-off
- You have a platform where you can reach a real person when something needs attention

**Choose a managed service if:**
- The founder or controller is stretched thin and compliance genuinely can't get attention
- You'd rather pay a premium for someone else to own the problem entirely
- State correspondence, deadline tracking, and registration management need to live outside your team
- Peace of mind is worth more to you than the cost difference

## The risk profile of each model

Neither model eliminates compliance risk, it just allocates it differently.

**Software risk:** If something goes wrong (a return doesn't file, a state changes a rule), you'll catch it through monitoring, or you won't catch it until a notice arrives. The better platforms have US-based support you can reach when this happens. The worse ones have ticket queues and 3–5 day response times.

**Managed service risk:** You're trusting another team's execution. Numeral's G2 reviews include documented cases of filing inaccuracies, late registrations, and onboarding described as "chaos, no organization" — despite a penalty guarantee. The guarantee covers the penalty; it doesn't prevent the error or the stress of discovering it after the fact.

The guarantee framing can create a false sense that errors won't happen. Both models require some oversight; the question is how much.

## How company size and growth stage affect the decision

| Stage | Typical fit |
|---|---|
| Pre-revenue or early stage (1–2 states) | Software; low cost, manageable complexity |
| Growing ($2M–$10M, 3–8 states) | Software with CSP enrollment if SST-eligible; managed service if ops capacity is zero |
| Scaling ($10M–$50M, 10–20 states) | Software with CSP enrollment becomes significantly cheaper; managed service cost escalates |
| Enterprise ($50M+, complex structure) | Enterprise software (Avalara) or full-service tax firm; neither consumer-grade option fits |

## The SST wildcard

If your business qualifies as a remote seller under SST rules and you have nexus in SST member states, the software-vs-managed-service decision has a specific financial dimension: only software platforms with CSP status can offer state-funded free filing in SST states. Managed services don't participate in the CSP program, so they charge full price regardless.

For a seller with 10 SST states filing monthly, the difference between a CSP platform ($0 for those filings) and Numeral ($750/month) is $9,000/year, every year, recurring. At that math, even a seller who values the managed service model should run the numbers.

## The bottom line

If you have any ops capacity at all, even one person who can spend an hour a month on compliance oversight, software is almost always the more cost-efficient choice, especially if SST enrollment applies. Managed services make sense when the fully-hands-off model is genuinely worth the premium, and when you've vetted the provider's execution record carefully.
